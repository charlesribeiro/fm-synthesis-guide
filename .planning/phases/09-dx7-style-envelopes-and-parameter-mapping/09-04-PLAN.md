<objective>
Give a human the controls needed to hear what per-operator envelopes actually do,
gate the phase on that listening pass and on Lesson 6 still completing against
the envelope-driven engine, then complete the phase validation record.

Purpose: the automated suites prove the envelope state machine is arithmetically
correct, bounded and finite, and that no malformed payload reaches the render
thread. They cannot prove a release is click-free, that an attack sounds like an
attack rather than a fade, that a modulator's envelope is audibly shaping timbre,
or that the engine is genuinely silent at rest. This phase removed the one
amplitude ramp a human had already approved at two prior checkpoints and replaced
it with six independent ones; the automated suite alone is not sufficient
confidence for that.

Output: an extended dev harness, an approved blocking listening checkpoint, any
tuning it asks for applied, and a completed validation record.
</objective>

<task type="checkpoint:human-verify" gate="blocking">
  <name>Task 2: Blocking listening checkpoint — silence at rest, click-free note lifecycle, audible per-operator timbral evolution, bounded worst case, and the Lesson 6 regression</name>

  <what-built>
Phase 9 replaced the single global click-prevention ramp on a dedicated Web Audio
gain node with six independent DX7-style four-rate/four-level envelope generators
running inside the DSP kernel, one per operator, each scaling that operator's own
block before it is summed as a carrier or read as a modulation source. The
worklet gained its first note-lifecycle message: note-on and note-off now reach
the kernel as a validated gate message carrying velocity, which is where velocity
scaling moved to. Envelope rates are DX7-style — a rate is speed toward the
current segment's target from wherever the level currently sits, so a release
mid-attack or a retrigger mid-release moves smoothly from the actual current
level with no snap to a fixed starting value. The Algorithm 1 lesson's starting
patch now gives its carriers a sustained envelope and its modulators a decaying
one. All of that is automated-green, including a bounded-and-finite sweep across
a full note lifecycle on every taxonomy group at maximum feedback and maximum
level. What no automated test can reach is whether any of it sounds right,
because jsdom implements no Web Audio API at all.
  </what-built>

  <how-to-verify>
**Setup.** Use headphones, start at a low volume, raise gradually. Run
`npm run start:harness` and open `http://localhost:4200/dev/worklet-harness.html`.
Click "Enable audio" first — nothing plays before that gesture.

**Why this checkpoint matters more than the last two:** the amplitude ramp you
approved at the 05-04 and 07-03 checkpoints has been deleted. Click safety on
every note-on and note-off in this app now depends entirely on the shapes you are
about to listen to. Silence at rest also lost its dedicated gain node — nothing
holds the graph quiet any more except the envelopes reaching zero.

**Check 1 — Silence at rest.** Enable audio and then do nothing for at least ten
seconds. Expect complete silence: no tone, no hum, no faint pad. Then select each
of the four envelope presets in turn, still without playing, and confirm silence
holds through every preset change. Report anything audible, however quiet.

**Check 2 — Default envelope, click-free lifecycle.** Select the default preset
and an algorithm from the additive-stacks group. Play, hold for a couple of
seconds, stop. Repeat several times at different speeds. Expect a clean onset with
no tick or pop, a steady held tone, and a clean decay to silence with no click at
the end and no audible tail cut-off. This is the direct replacement for the ramp
you previously approved — judge it against that memory.

**Check 3 — Slow swell.** Select the slow-swell preset and play. Expect an
unmistakable rise into the note over a noticeable time, not an instant onset with
a fade. Confirm the rise sounds smooth rather than stepped or grainy — a stepped
or zipper-like quality would mean the envelope is updating per block rather than
per sample and should be reported.

**Check 4 — Percussive decay.** Select the percussive preset and hold a note.
Expect a strong attack that falls quickly to a quieter sustain and then stays
there for as long as you hold it, rather than continuing to fade to nothing.
Release and confirm a clean ending. The "stays there" part is the sustain plateau
— if it keeps decaying to silence while held, report it.

**Check 5 — Per-operator timbral evolution (D-01, the point of the phase).**
Select an algorithm with real modulation depth — anything from the tree/branch or
rooting group — and choose the carrier-sustains / modulator-decays preset. Hold a
long note. Expect the tone to open bright and metallic and to mellow noticeably
over the first second or so while still sounding at a steady loudness: the
modulators are fading, the carriers are not. Compare directly against the default
preset on the same algorithm, where the timbre should stay constant for the whole
held note. If both presets sound identical in timbre over time, the modulators'
envelopes are not reaching the output — report that specifically, it is the single
most important negative result this checkpoint can produce.

**Check 6 — Mid-segment release and retrigger (D-04).** With the slow-swell preset
selected, start a note and stop it well before the swell finishes. Expect the sound
to fall away smoothly from whatever level it had reached — no jump up, no jump
down, no click. Then start a note, stop it, and immediately start it again while
the release is still audible. Expect it to rise from where it was, not from
silence and not with a click. Do each at least three times.

**Check 7 — Held-note re-patch.** Start a note and, while it is sounding, change
the algorithm select; then change the feedback slider; then change the envelope
preset. Expect the character to change each time without the note stopping,
restarting, or re-attacking from silence. An audible re-attack on any of the three
means envelope state is being reset where it should not be. Then stop and confirm
complete silence — no stuck, sounding voice.

**Check 8 — Bounded worst case.** Raise feedback to maximum, engage the
maximum-operator-level control, and play with the default preset. Expect it to
sound harsh and edgy — that is intended and is what the routing phase taught. What
you are judging is whether it stays bounded: it must not be painfully loud, must
not keep getting louder the longer you hold it, and must not produce silence,
clicking or digital garbage. Report its loudness relative to Check 2.

**Check 9 — The app itself.** Keep the harness running. In a second terminal run
`npm start -- --port 4201` and open `http://localhost:4201/playground` (a distinct
port so it does not collide with the harness already on 4200). Enable audio and play the on-screen keyboard
and the computer-keyboard mapping. Confirm the notes sound at a comfortable level
comparable to before this phase, are click-free on both press and release, stop
cleanly, and leave silence when you stop playing. Play several notes in fast
succession and confirm each retrigger is clean.

**Check 10 — Lesson 6 regression (D-03).** Open
`http://localhost:4200/learn/algorithm-1`. Hold a note and confirm the tone now
audibly mellows over its life while continuing to sound — the lesson's starting
patch was given the carrier-sustains / modulator-decays treatment. Then perform the
lesson's stated try-this action and trigger a note. Confirm the completion state
still fires, the described sonic change is still audible, and the try-this guidance
still matches what you hear.

**Check 11 — Honesty copy.** Confirm the persistent educational-approximation
label still appears and still reads exactly as before. Envelopes becoming real
licenses no stronger claim; report any wording anywhere in the app, the README or
the architecture document that now overstates what the engine is.
  </how-to-verify>
