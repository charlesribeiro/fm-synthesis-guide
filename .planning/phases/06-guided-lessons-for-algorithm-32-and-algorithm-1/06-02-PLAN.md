**The row.** id `'algorithm-1'`, `algorithmId` 1, title "A stack and a tower". The objective states
that the learner will hear two independent voices inside one algorithm and prove they are
independent by changing one and leaving the other alone. Write two to three original explanation
paragraphs covering: that algorithm 1 reaches output through two separate paths rather than one;
that one path is a short pair where a single operator shapes its partner; that the other is a
deeper chain where each operator shapes the next before the last one reaches output, with the
topmost operator also feeding back into itself; and why a deeper chain produces a brighter, more
complex timbre than a pair at the same levels. Describe the shape, not the operator numbers — the
page derives and displays which operators are carriers from `derive-role.ts`. Do not transcribe or
paraphrase Yamaha manual text, Dexed comments, or any third-party diagram caption.

**The starting patch.** Build it as its own new object by spreading
`DEFAULT_OPERATOR_PARAMETERS` per operator; never mutate `DEFAULT_PATCH` or
`DEFAULT_OPERATOR_PARAMETERS`. Set `algorithmId` to 1. Leave every operator's `ratio` at 1 so the
timbre difference the lesson teaches comes from the routing depth rather than from detuned
partials. Give the two carriers an `outputLevel` of 75 so both voices are clearly audible; give
the modulator feeding the short pair an `outputLevel` of 60; and give the three operators of the
deeper chain descending `outputLevel` values of 55, 50 and 45 from the operator nearest output to
the one furthest from it, so the chain is bright without being harsh. Set `feedback` to 3 — a
mid-scale value on the 0..7 depth scale, non-zero so the self-loop the explanation describes is
actually audible. Determine which operator id fills each of those roles by reading Algorithm 1's
edge list in `algorithms.ts` and the derivation rules in `derive-role.ts`; do not assume the
mapping from this prose. Freeze the patch and its operator record at every level.

**The try-this step.** `targetOperator` is the middle operator of the deeper chain — the one that
both receives modulation and passes it on — `targetParam` is `'ratio'`, `direction` is
`'increase'`. Its `instruction` tells the learner to raise that operator's frequency ratio; its
`expectedEffect` describes the tower voice growing brighter and more metallic while the pair voice
stays exactly where it was, which is what proves the two paths are independent.

**The spec.** Write `lessons.spec.ts` covering every case in `<behavior>`, iterating over `LESSONS`
rather than asserting against a hardcoded row count, so Phase 11's thirty further rows inherit the
same gate for free. Follow `algorithms.spec.ts`'s precedent for the two cross-check assertions:
populate the expected carrier set and feedback operator for algorithm 1 by hand from
`algorithms.ts`'s edge list and compare them against `deriveCarriers`/`getFeedbackOperator`, so a
transcription slip in either the dataset or the lesson fails a named test rather than shipping
silently. The reachability invariant is the one genuinely new assertion — it exists so no future
lesson can declare a try-this step the learner has no room to perform.
